# Stage Shift Level Art

Read [Level Design and Level Art](../../../Docs/LevelDesignAndArt.md), [Art Direction](../../../Docs/ArtDirection.md), and the nearest finished location. Use [Stage Shift Level Design](../stage-shift-level-design/SKILL.md) for routes, camera transitions, and controls. Apply visual-quality-control for new meshes and materials.

1. Capture the current gameplay view and inspect close views of exact joints. Use the user's reference and a production counterpart. Generated concepts guide composition and construction. Read scale, pivots, mounting geometry, and materials from the project.
2. Inventory visible kit pieces and production mechanisms before authoring. Preserve their materials and anchors. For a gap in the kit, place a matching purpose-built piece and trace its editable source. Keep existing unrelated actors out of the edit.
3. Build walkable finishes continuously along the whole width of joins. Check both sides, plank direction, edge heights, visible coverage, and collision independently. Decorative seam relief should end in visible backing. Use the actual mesh surface to place each freestanding prop. Check feet, wall brackets, overhead fixings, and local shadows from ground level. Move a room cluster by its intended supports, preserving contact and usable orientation.
4. Review a matched wide before/after and detail views of every edited joint, support and mount. Inspect both doorway approaches and each side of paired hardware. A lamp needs a shelf or bracket that reaches the wall. A curtain needs a visible load path from fabric through hangers to a beam or rail, with clear hems and tiebacks. Check from the side so an overlap in projection cannot hide a gap. Resolve each visual failure and repeat the affected captures before acceptance.
